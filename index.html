HOW TO POUR

  • Grab the bottle by its neck to move it, by its body to tip it — you control the flow completely. Scroll to tip it too.
  • Or hold SPACE / HOLD to tip it, or AUTO to pour the set amount.
  • Drag the glass (or ← →) to tilt it — pour down the side for less foam.
  • Ice, Mentos, salt and the spoon are in the ADD row. Press ? for every key.

ACHIEVEMENTS

    WHAT IS SIMULATED

    The 3-D view ray-casts the glass, liquid, foam and every ice cube at its real depth (press V for the flat cut-away section). Bulk liquid is well-mixed (one CO₂ concentration, one temperature); every bubble is tracked as a weighted particle; the head is a stack of foam layers, each with its own gas, liquid and dissolved CO₂.

    1. Solubility — Henry's law c_eq = K₀(T)·P with the Weiss (1974) fit ln K₀ = −58.0931 + 90.5069·(100/T) + 22.2940·ln(T/100) mol/(kg·atm), × an estimated Sechenov salting-out for sugar.
    2. Supersaturation — S = c/c_eq − 1. Altitude lowers P; warmth lowers k_H.
    3. Critical radius — R* = 2σ / (c/k_H − P_atm − ρgz): hydrostatic depth suppresses nucleation.
    4. Homogeneous nucleation — J = J₀·exp(−16πσ³ / 3k_BTΔP²) ≈ 10^(−millions): impossible, so all bubbles are heterogeneous.
    5. Active sites — N_a = N₀ · (θ/180°) · (1 µm / R*)²: power-law cavity sizes × Bankoff gas-trapping by contact angle θ.
    6. Detachment — R_d = min(5r_c, ∛(1.5 r_c σ sinθ/ρg), r_c σ sinθ / 3μu): pinch-off, buoyancy or flow-drag beats contact-line pinning.
    7. Site frequency — exact integral of dR/dt = (D/R + k_c)·RTΔc/P: t = [R_d/k_c − (D/k_c²)·ln(1 + k_cR_d/D)] / a, with Levich convective k_c.
    8. Wetting-front entrapment — dry rough surfaces (frosty ice, styrofoam, Mentos) trap V_air = A_new · δ of air as seed bubbles.
    9. Bubble growth — dn/dt = 4πR²·k_L·(c − k_H·p_CO₂); radius from (P_L + 2σ/R)·⁴⁄₃πR³ = nRT (Newton solve). Entrained air bubbles start with p_CO₂ = 0.
    10. Mass transfer — Sh = 2 + 0.6Re^½Sc^⅓ (rigid) ↔ 2 + 1.13(ReSc)^½ (mobile); turbulence adds 0.4(εν)^¼Sc^−½.
    11. Rise — Schiller–Naumann drag between Stokes (24/Re) and Hadamard (16/Re) limits, Tomiyama cap (8/3)Eo/(Eo+4), plus bubble-swarm drift 0.35√(gαH).
    12. The pour — free fall v = √(v₀² + 2gh), necking d = d₀√(v₀/v); air entrainment (Bin 1993) Q_a/Q = 0.04·Fr^0.28·(h/d₀)^0.4; plume u_c = 5.8·v·d/z (Pope); transit loss through the stream and wall film (area/volume)·4√(Dt/π) (Higbie, Nusselt film); entrainment scaled ×0.35, fitted to Liger-Belair et al. 2010; bubble size cap (Hinze) d_max = 0.725(σ/ρ)^0.6 ε^−0.4.
    13. Foam — drainage q = k·ρg/μ with measured permeabilities k = 8·10⁻⁴D²φ² (rigid) ↔ 6.7·10⁻⁴D²φ^{3/2} (mobile); coarsening d⟨R²⟩/dt = 4σD·He/(P·h)·(1−√(φ/φ*))²; films rupture once drainage dries the foam below a critical liquid fraction: rate ∝ exp[3(φc/φ − 1)] (Carrier & Colin); capillary pressure Pc = σ/(R√(φ/0.33)) shown as a diagnostic; foam liquid degasses over τ = r_pb²/4D.
    14. Drinks — each drink's sugar (°Brix) sets viscosity μ/μ_w ≈ e^{0.0287·Bx}, density, heat capacity and Sechenov salting-out of CO₂; its surfactants set σ, bubble-skin mobility and φc. Carbonation levels are sourced (see README).
    15. Ice cubes — rigid bodies (gravity, buoyancy, drag C_d 1.05, stacking) that float, slide when tilted and overflow onto the table; each melts by its own wetted area: warm to the drink's freezing point, then m = q/L_f; Churchill natural convection with water's 4 °C density maximum; soda below its freezing point freezes onto the ice; meltwater dilutes CO₂ and sugar.
    16. Hand and slosh — the cup follows your tilt like a critically damped hand; the drink's first mode ω² = (gξ₁/R)·tanh(ξ₁h/R) (Lamb, ~3.5 Hz) chases tan δ = −a_x/(g + a_y). Liquid over the rim clings below ~0.3 m/s (teapot effect) and is thrown above it.
    17. Erupting foam — leaves the mouth at u = foam rate / mouth area; flung off when ρ_f·u² beats its yield stress and rim grip, otherwise it oozes down the outside.
    18. Stream and table — the stream flies as parcels (breaks up after 19.5·d·[√We(1+3Oh)]^0.85, Grant & Middleman); puddles spread as r ∝ t^⅛ (Huppert) to ~3 mm thick (2·l_c·sin(θ/2)) and merge.
    19. Spray and spills — bursting bubbles fling jet and film drops centimetres up; foam that overflows faster than a Bingham film can creep down the glass breaks off in clumps; on the table it heaps (yield stress, h² = 2τ_y(r₀ − r)/ρ_f g) and puddles pin at the table's edge until thicker than 2·l_c·sin(α/2).
    20. Salt, Mentos and ice as bodies — gravity, buoyancy, drag and the swing of the cup (−(α×r − ω²r), Coriolis): a flick throws them out. Salt dissolves at k·A·ρ_w·(m_sat − m) (Ranz–Marshall), faster stirred, stopping at saturation (~6.1 mol/kg); on ice it melts brine and chills the cube.
    21. Meltwater layer — fresh meltwater floats on sugary or salty soda: no CO₂ (a lid on the fizz), no surfactants (weaker foam), ice in it melts slowly; turbulence mixes it in at u_e = 0.1·u/Ri (Turner).
    22. Salt and melting — ice melts at the solution's freezing point T_f = −i·K_f·m (NaCl i ≈ 1.87); salt lowers it, so ice melts faster, and salts out CO₂: k_H × 10^(−0.11·[NaCl]) (Sechenov; Weisenberger & Schumpe 1996). Dissolving it takes in heat (+3.88 kJ/mol); brine is denser and holds less heat.
    23. Surface tension at the rim — meniscus l_c√(2(1−sinθ)); liquid pins at the rim up to √2·l_c ≈ 3.9 mm above it (Gibbs); foam domes until its yield stress 0.51(σ/R)(φ_g−0.635)² gives way, then creeps down the outside.
    24. Ice and the pour — the stream cascades through the pile to the liquid: full-drop jet energy, entrainment × 2 + 1.5·(cube-heights fallen), bubbles carried down the gaps; melting releases the ~2.5 % air frozen into the ice; cooling raises solubility ~10 %, a minor brake.
    25. Stirring — turbulence ε ≈ 5 W/kg, a moving steel spoon surface shedding bubbles at 0.3 m/s, and stroke air entrainment Q ≈ ½·w·u·δ.
    26. The bottle — flow is not set directly: the liquid surface in the tilted bottle gives the head over the lip, and air must get back in. Smooth: weir flow out, orifice air in, headspace suction balances them. Flooded neck: air only as bubbles rising at 0.35√(gd), capping flow at (πd²/4)·0.35√(gd) ≈ Clanet & Searby's (π/12)√g·d^{5/2} — glug-glug. The jet leaves the lip along the axis and falls ballistically.
    27. Tilt — the liquid keeps a horizontal surface in the rotated cup: equivalent level z ↔ world height h by cross-section area D·z = A_below(h); spill at the low rim; surface width ∝ 1/cos θ.
    28. Heat — lumped soda, cup wall (U = 1/(1/h + t/2k)), ice (sensible heat, then melting at 334 kJ/kg), room air, and fizz cooling of ~20 kJ per mol CO₂ released.
    29. Ice geometry — cubes pack in layers; the pack rests on the floor until Archimedes buoyancy exceeds its weight, then floats with draft m/ρA.
